\noindent{\bf Abstract:}\\
In this paper, we consider a cut point $M^*$ for  number of failure times in the stress component of a  multi-component stress-strength system based on  progressively Type-II censoring  assumed  as  a truncated geometry  distribution while the number of failure times in strength component is assumed  fixed. The optimal schemes and the cut points   can be considered and the most important is  determination them so that the total cost of the test  and  the  estimation of multi-component stress-strength reliability have been reached to minimum  and maximum, respectively. A simulation study is carried out to check which different censoring schemes and cut points   have the best performance in terms of the total cost  and the estimation of  multi-component stress-strength reliability. At the end,  a real example  is given to illustrate the  proposed method.\\
\noindent{\bf Keywords:} 
Multi-component stress-strength reliability,   Type-II  right censoring, Truncated geometry distribution. \\
\noindent{\bf Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):} $\rm 62N01$, $\rm 62N05$. \\

\section{\bf Introduction and Preliminaries}\label{section1}
In medical, engineering and industrial applications, due to time and cost restrictions in lifetime data, censoring arises when exact lifetimes are only partially known. There are various types of censoring such as Type-I and II right censoring, doubly Type-II censoring, random censoring and progressive Type-I and Type-II censoring, etc. On the other hand, in reliability literature, the stress-strength model described as the reliability of a unit or a system in terms of the stress random variable $X$ and strength random variable $Y$ . The system fails if the stress is greater than the strength, and the probability $R = P(X < Y )$ is the reliability of the system (\cite{Kotz2003}).

A system that consists of more than one component is called a multi-component system. The multi-component stress-strength reliability is defined as the probability that at least $s$ out of $k$ independent and identically distributed ($i.i.d$) strength components survive a common random stress. This model was first introduced by \cite{BhattacharyyaJohnson1974} and has since been extensively studied due to its wide applicability in fields such as civil engineering, aerospace, and manufacturing \cite{Rao2013}.

A combination of Type-II right censoring and multi-component stress-strength reliability can be descibed as follows. Let $k$ random variables $Z_1,Z_2,\ldots,Z_k$ be $i.i.d$  random variables with continuous cdf $F_{Z_j}(z_j)$ which are subjected to a common random stress $T$, an independent random variable with continuous cdf $F_T (t)$.

In recent years, several authors have investigated stress-strength reliability under progressive censoring schemes. For instance, \cite{Asgharzadeh2017} studied estimation of $R=P(X<Y)$ under  Type-II right  censoring for the Weibull distribution. 
\cite{Jha2020} considered the problem of estimating multi-component stress-strength (MSS) reliability under progressive Type II censoring when stress and strength variables follow unit Gompertoz distributions with common scale parameter. They estimated MSS reliability under frequentist and Bayesian approaches.
\cite{El-Desokey2026} developed a reliability framework for multi-component stress–strength systems by modeling stress and strength using the Burr–Hatke distribution under progressive Type II censoring. Maximum likelihood estimators were derived and the stress–strength reliability function, along with their asymptotic confidence intervals.\\
The novelty of this work lies in assuming that the number of failure times in the strength component is random variable from the truncated geometric distribution. 
\section{\bf Main Results}\label{section2}
Let $Z_{i1:n_1}\leq \ldots \leq Z_{ik:n_1},\ \ i = 1,\ldots,m,$  denote a Type-II right  censored sample from the exponential distribution with parameter $\lambda_1$, i.e.  $f(z_{ij})=\lambda_1\exp(-\lambda_1 z_{ij});\    \   \lambda_1>0\ \ j=1,\ldots,k,$  under  the Type-II right censoring as well as let $T_{1:n_2}\leq,\ldots,\leq T_{m:n_2}$ be another such sample taken from the exponential distribution with parameter $\lambda_2$
under the Type-II right censoring.  Then the reliability of a multi-component stress-strength model is written as \\
$G_{s,k} = P$(at least s   of the  $(Z_1,\ldots , Z_k)$  exceed $T$)=
$\sum_{p=s}^{k} {k\choose p}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left[ 1-F_Z(t)\right]^p\left[F_Z(t)\right]^{k-p} f_T(t)\,d{t}$.


Here, let $k$  be fixed and $M$ (the number of failures in stress model) be  distributed as the truncated geometric distribution at points  $M^*$ and $0$ 
\begin{eqnarray}\label{M1}
P(M=m)=\frac{\theta (1-\theta)^m}{P(1\leq M\leq M^*)}=\frac{\theta (1-\theta)^m}{(1-\theta-(1-\theta)^{M^*+1})};\ m=1,2,\cdots, M^*
\end{eqnarray}

Then, the likelihood function of $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$ and $\theta$   expressed as
\begin{eqnarray}\label{M2}
L(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\theta)=f(Z_{i1},\ldots, Z_{ik})=\sum_{m=1}^{M^*} f(z_{ij}|M=m)P(M=m),
\end{eqnarray}

in which, we know that
\begin{eqnarray}\label{M3}\nonumber
f(z_{ij}|M=m)&=&C_1\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[C_2 \prod_{j=1}^{k} f(Z_{ij})(1-F(Z_{ik})^{(n_1-k)}\right ] \\ &&\times  f(t_i) \left[1-F(t_m)\right]^{(n_2-m)}, \ \  \ C_1=\frac{n_2!}{(n_2-m)!},\ \   C_2=\frac{n_1!}{(n_1-k)!}.
\end{eqnarray}

%\par In the last few years, many researchers investigated the estimation of the multi-component stress-strength reliability (MSSR) system using different distributions based on complete  and censored sample data.

In this paper, we first estimate the multi-component stress-strength reliability function by estimating the three parameters $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$ and $\theta$. Then, we examine the designs that maximize the estimated multi-component reliability function, which is a function of $\hat{\lambda_1}$, $\hat{\lambda_2}$ and $\hat{\theta}$ as well as  minimizing the  introduced cost function.

Then, the likelihood function of $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$ and $\theta$ is given by
\begin{eqnarray}\label{M5}\\ \nonumber 
L(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \theta)&=& \frac{\theta}{(1-\theta-(1-\theta)^{M^*+1})}\sum_{m=1}^{M^*}C_1(1-\theta)^{m} C_2^m \lambda_1^{km}\\   \nonumber &&\times e^{\left(-\lambda_1\left[\sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^k z_{ij}+(n_1-k)\sum_{i=1}^m z_{ik}\right]\right)} \lambda_2^{m}
 e^{\left(-\lambda_2\left[\sum_{i=1}^m t_i+m t_m(n_2-m)\right] \right)}.
\end{eqnarray}
After simplification, calculating the first partial derivatives of \eqref{M5} with respect to  $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$ and $\theta$ along with equating to zero,  the likelihood equations do not have explicit solutions. Therefore, we  use  the numerical   solution to solve them. After getting maximum likelihood  estimators  $\widehat{\lambda_1}$, $\widehat{\lambda_2}$  and $\widehat{\theta}$ from likelihood equations, we  substitute them in 
\begin{eqnarray}\label{M7}\nonumber
G_{s,k}&=&The\  probability\  of\  stress-strength= P(at\  least\  s\    of\  the\ Z_j> T; {j=1,2,\cdots, k})\\ \nonumber &=& \sum_{p=s}^{k} {k\choose p}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left[ 1-F_Z(t)\right]^p\left[F_Z(t)\right]^{k-p} f_T(t)\,d{t}\\ \nonumber &=& \sum_{p=s}^{k} {k\choose p}\int_{0}^{+\infty} e^{-\lambda_1 t p} \left[1-e^{-\lambda_1 t}\right]^{k-p}\lambda_2 e^{-\lambda_2 t}\,d{t}\\ \nonumber &=& 
 \sum_{p=s}^{k} {k\choose p}\int_{0}^{+\infty} \lambda_2 e^{-(\lambda_2+\lambda_1 p)t} \left[1-e^{-\lambda_1 t}\right]^{k-p}\,d{t},\\
\end{eqnarray}
and we can  get $\widehat{G_{s,k}}$ which is a function of $M^*$, $\widehat{\lambda_1}$, $\widehat{\lambda_2}$ and $\widehat{\theta}$. Then, the $\widehat{G_{s,k}}$ can be calculated. The total  cost function can be rewritten as (see \cite{BasiriBeigi2020})
\begin{eqnarray}\label{M8} 
E(T_C)=C_0+C_t E(T_{m:n_2})+C_un_2,
\end{eqnarray}
which $C_0$  is the cost of setting up a test and other related costs as well as  $C_t$ and $C_u$ are  time-dependent cost on test and cost per unit under test, respectively. Also,  $T_{m:n_2}$ is  $m^{\rm th}$ (last)  order statistic of   $(T_{1:n_2},\ldots, T_{m:n_2})$ from  Type-II right censored sample taken under the exponential distribution with parameter  $\lambda_2$ parameter.

In order to compute $E(T_{m:n_2})$, let $f(t_i)=\lambda_2 e^{-\lambda_2 t_i};\ \lambda_2>0$, by appropriate transformations (see \cite{DavidNagaraja2003}),

The optimal censoring plan in Type-II right censoring and optimal $M^*$ based on  minimum of final cost function \eqref{M12} and maximum   of $\widehat{G_{s,k}}$ calculated by  \eqref{M7} is considered.
\section{\bf Simulation Study}\label{section3}
In this section, the results of previous section  are presented. We consider $C_0=20;\  C_t=3; \  C_u=12$. Based on  $3$ different schemes of   Type-II right  censored sample with size $m$ taken from the exponential distribution with $\lambda_2$ and $3$ other schemes of Type-II right  censored  sample with size $k$ taken from the exponential distribution with $\lambda_1$,  which censored sample  is optimal in terms of   minimizing  cost function and   maximizing of $\widehat{G_{s,k}}$.
\begin{alg}\label{alg1}
 This algorithm has the following steps:
\begin{itemize}
\item
Step 1:  
   First, for given primary and arbitrary  values of the parameter  $\lambda_2$ from the exponential distribution  a Type-II right censored sample with sample size $m$ is generated.\\
\item 
Step 2:
Again,  for given primary and arbitrary  values of the parameter  $\lambda_1$ from the exponential distribution, a Type-II right  censored sample with different sample sizes of $k$ is generated.\\
\item
Step 3: 
Using "optim" in R,  compute the  maximum likelihood estimates of $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$ and $\theta$.\\
\item 
Step 4:   
 By substitution of $\widehat{\lambda_1}$, $\widehat{\lambda_2}$ and $\widehat{\theta}$  get  $\widehat{G_{s,k}}$.\\
\item
Step 5: 
After calculation of the  $\widehat{G_{s,k}}$, acquire  the  objective function $Obj=\frac{\widehat{G_{s,k}}}{Cost}$ (see page 236 from \cite{PakdamanAhmadi2019} ).\\
\item 
Step 6:
Replicate the above processes 1000 times and report  the  best $M^*$  that has minimum   objective function for different quantities of $l$, $s$ for  some Type-II   censored samples.\\
\end{itemize}
\end{alg}

\section*{Discussion and Conclusion} 
In this paper, we introduced the concept of determining an optimal value for $M^*$, the cut point of  the number of failure times in the stress component of a  multi-component stress-strength system based on  Type-II censoring  assumed  as truncated geometry  distribution while the number of failure times in strength component is assumed  fixed. Also,  the optimal scheme  along with cut points can be considered and are  determined in such a way that the total cost of the test  and  the  estimation of multi-component stress-strength reliability have been reached to minimum  and maximum, respectively. A simulation study  results that in order to control $M^*$, different $s$'s  for special censoring schemes can be applied. This method can be generalized to some other censoring types such as progressive Type-II censoring schemes etc.
