\section{Effective Analysis of Genetic Diversity of Plant Species
through Artificial Intelligence
Tools}

\textbf{Naser Sabaghnia}\footnote{\begin{quote}
  \textbf{Corresponding Author:} sabaghnia@maragheh.ac.ir
  \end{quote}}

\begin{quote}

\textbf{Abstract:}

Genetic variation within species arises from complex interactions
between evolutionary processes and environmental factors, often
reflected in polymorphic patterns of Single-Nucleotide Polymorphisms
(SNPs). Traditional landscape genetics approaches have primarily focused
on either broad-scale or local-scale environmental effects, limiting the
statistical inference of genetic diversity. In this study, we present a
comprehensive statistical framework integrating supervised machine
learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and simulation-based
modeling to quantify the influence of multiple factors on genetic
variation. Supervised machine learning allows prediction of key genetic
metrics, such as allelic richness and population differentiation, while
simultaneously estimating the relative importance of environmental and
historical drivers. CNNs process SNP alignments as high-dimensional
data, extracting patterns without requiring predefined summary
statistics or population assignments. Simulation-based approaches,
including Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC), enable robust
estimation of parameter posteriors under complex evolutionary scenarios.
By combining these methods, our framework captures both discrete and
continuous genetic processes, models stochasticity in natural
populations, and provides interpretable statistical measures of factor
importance. This approach enhances inference across spatial and temporal
scales, facilitating hypothesis testing and evolutionary interpretation.
The framework also has practical applications for conservation planning
and biodiversity management, allowing researchers to identify
populations and environmental factors most critical for maintaining
genetic diversity. This integrative, statistics-driven approach
represents a significant advancement in molecular ecology, offering
scalable, interpretable, and robust tools for analyzing genomic
variation in natural populations.

\textbf{Keywords:} Supervised machine learning, Convolutional neural
networks, Statistical genomics

\section{Introduction}

Quantitative understanding of genetic variation in spatial and
environmental contexts is a central objective of landscape genetics.
Historically, molecular studies used organellar genes or allelic markers
such as microsatellites to quantify variation, while sequence-based data
informed deeper evolutionary inference (Holderegger \& Wagner, 2008).
Single-Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNP) data has revolutionized
statistical genetics by providing dense, genome-wide markers that enable
hypothesis testing at multiple scales (Eaton \& Overcast, 2020). Despite
these advances, selecting appropriate analytical methods remains a key
statistical challenge. Classical population genetics models often assume
discrete populations, migration-drift equilibrium, and neutrality, which
may not hold in complex landscapes. Mis-specification of populations or
unrecognized continuous processes can bias parameter estimation and
reduce statistical power. Similarly, approximate Bayesian computation
(ABC) and related model-based approaches require careful selection of
summary statistics, which can influence posterior estimates and
confidence intervals (Beaumont et al., 2002).

To overcome these limitations, machine learning methods provide flexible
statistical tools that integrate multiple data types, model nonlinear
interactions, and quantify variable importance. By combining simulations
with supervised learning, researchers can train models to identify
factors most strongly associated with genetic variation while
controlling for stochastic effects. Similarly, convolutional neural
networks (CNNs) allow the statistical extraction of patterns from DNA
sequence alignments treated as high-dimensional data, enabling inference
without pre-defined summary statistics or population assignments (Flagel
et al., 2019).

\section{Supervised machine learning in genetic
analysis}

Supervised machine learning (ML) is a statistical framework that maps
input features (e.g., environmental variables, SNP data) to outcomes
(e.g., allelic richness, heterozygosity) based on labeled datasets. Key
statistical steps included data preprocessing (normalization, encoding
of categorical variables, and missing data imputation), model selection
(algorithms such as random forests, gradient boosting machines, or
support vector machines), training and validation (k-fold
cross-validation or bootstrapping to estimate model performance metrics
like RMSE, R2), and inference estimation of feature importance using
permutation tests or Gini impurity to identify key drivers of genetic
variation). This approach allows quantitative integration of
environmental and historical factors, while simulations can expand
training datasets to include scenarios not observed in empirical
samples, increasing statistical robustness.

Integrating discrete environmental factors within a common analytical
framework allows researchers to pinpoint the elements that significantly
influence genetic diversity in biological species. This integration
could take several forms, such as conducting independent polymorphic
genetic analyses and synthesizing the results statistically, or
performing a single comprehensive analysis that incorporates
environmental factors. One potential approach involves developing a full
likelihood framework, though this is a complex and daunting task due to
the intricate models required (Beaumont et al., 2002). Alternatively,
simulation-based methods that generate genetic data in a spatial context
offer a promising direction. Despite the challenges of applying such
frameworks to empirical systems; especially those with poorly
characterized life parameters like generation length and mutation rates,
the integration of simulations within a supervised machine learning
framework could streamline the process. Supervised machine learning, a
subset of artificial intelligence, involves training a predictive model
using pre-classified datasets. In this context, a model would be trained
on datasets where only historical or landscape factors are evident.
Given that both types of factors usually coexist, pretraining can also
utilize data reflecting the presence of both factors. This approach can
simplify the development of a framework capable of identifying the
factors most influential in shaping genetic diversity in the focal
species (Schrider and Kern, 2018). Integrating discrete environmental
factors under a supervised machine learning framework offers a robust
approach to understanding the processes shaping genetic diversity in
biological species. This method simplifies the complex task of model
development, providing a practical and efficient tool for researchers to
identify key influences on genetic variation. By bridging the gap
between historical and landscape genetics, this framework enhances our
ability to test hypotheses and interpret genetic data across diverse
spatial and temporal scales.

\section{Artificial neural network}

Integrating discrete environmental factors within a common analytical
framework is essential for identifying the influences on genetic
diversity in biological species. A promising approach involves the use
of convolutional neural networks, a type of artificial neural network
that mimics the human brain\textquotesingle s neural connections by
artificially replicating neuron interactions. Originally developed for
image and video classification, convolutional neural networks can be
adapted for genetic data analysis by altering data representation
methods (Flagel et al., 2019). This simulation-based framework, which
employs convolutional neural networks, offers a robust method for
analyzing genetic diversity. By adapting convolutional neural networks
to process genetic data as images of DNA sequence alignments,
researchers can avoid the limitations of traditional summary statistics
and a priori decisions. This approach provides a comprehensive tool for
identifying the environmental and historical factors that shape genetic
diversity, bridging the gap between different genetic analysis
disciplines and enhancing our understanding of genetic variation across
diverse spatial and temporal scales.

\section{Polymorphic genetics}

Evolutionary genetics is inherently challenging because each species has
a distinct history and responds uniquely to its environment. This
uniqueness adds layers of complexity to research, but it also enriches
the field by presenting a diverse array of questions to explore and
hypotheses to test. The interplay of factors such as life history,
habitat specificity, and ecological location shapes genetic patterns in
ways that are unique to each species. Taxon-specific traits
significantly influence genetic patterns, and trait-based approaches
have been instrumental in clarifying the nature of discordant genetic
patterns (Sullivan et al., 2019). Many species remain poorly understood
in terms of their ecology, evolution, distribution, and population
dynamics. This lack of knowledge complicates data analysis, as the
choice of analytical methods should ideally be guided by comprehensive
information about the focal species. Without such information,
researchers risk using inappropriate analytical methods, leading to
potentially flawed conclusions. Although numerous programs and
frameworks are available to address scientific questions, selecting the
right one depends on understanding the species\textquotesingle{} life
history, distribution, and population dynamics. Initially, polymorphic
genetics relied on various types of genetic data, including sequence
data based on gene genealogies. More recently, researchers have used
summary statistics, such as nucleotide diversity, calculated from
discrete units (Garrick et al., 2020). In contrast, earlier studies
based on allelic data, such as microsatellites, often analyzed metrics
among demes or individuals. The advent of Single-Nucleotide Polymorphism
data has revolutionized genetic research, as these data are economical
to generate, even in non-model systems. However, a significant
distinction remains in how genetic variation is partitioned, whether by
populations or individuals. This partitioning can impact the
interpretation of genetic patterns and the subsequent conclusions drawn
from the data.

The unique genetic patterns observed among taxonomically similar species
underscore the importance of taxon-specific traits. Understanding these
traits helps elucidate the reasons behind discordant genetic patterns
and provides insights into the evolutionary processes at play. Knowledge
gaps about a species\textquotesingle{} ecology, evolution, distribution,
and population dynamics have significant implications for the analysis
of genetic data. Accurate data interpretation requires an informed
selection of analytical methods, which in turn depends on comprehensive
species-specific information. Evolutionary genetics is a complex field
due to the unique histories and environmental responses of each species.
This complexity, while challenging, also makes research more intriguing
by offering a wide array of questions and hypotheses. The development
and application of appropriate analytical methods depend on a thorough
understanding of the species under study. Advances in genetic data
collection, such as the use of Single-Nucleotide Polymorphism, have
improved our ability to study genetic variation, but challenges remain
in accurately partitioning and interpreting this variation. By focusing
on taxon-specific traits and leveraging new technologies, researchers
can continue to uncover the intricate details of evolutionary genetics
and enhance our understanding of biodiversity.

\section{Approximate Bayesian
computation}

The complexity of evolutionary genetics requires advanced methods for
accurate data analysis. While traditional approaches like approximate
Bayesian computation rely heavily on researcher choices in summarizing
genetic variation, convolutional neural networks offer a promising
alternative. By using alignment images, convolutional neural networks
can enhance the capacity to infer both discrete and continuous genetic
processes without the biases inherent in traditional methods. The
approximate Bayesian computation allows researchers to make statistical
inferences based on simulated data that approximate the observed data.
However, the effectiveness of approximate Bayesian computation depends
on how researchers summarize genetic variation. Poor summarization can
reduce the statistical power of the analysis and lead to biased results.
This is especially problematic in phylogeography and landscape genetics,
where model-based methods have become prevalent over the last decades
(Zamudio et al., 2016). The choice of analytical model often reflects
the researchers\textquotesingle{} beliefs about the processes shaping
genetic variation. Since models are inherently simplified
representations of reality, they can lead to biased or erroneous
inferences, particularly if important factors like genetic drift,
selection, hybridization, and biological interactions are overlooked.
Ignoring environmental-level analyses can result in confirmation bias,
where findings align with preconceived notions rather than empirical
evidence. convolutional neural networks provide a robust alternative to
approximate Bayesian computation by processing genetic data as images of
DNA sequence alignments. This approach retains the essential features of
genetic diversity and does not require a priori decisions about
population structure or summary statistics. The convolutional neural
networks can capture both discrete and continuous genetic processes,
offering a more comprehensive and unbiased analysis framework. The
integration of convolutional neural networks into genetic data analysis
offers a robust solution to the limitations of traditional methods like
approximate Bayesian computation. By processing genetic data as images,
convolutional neural networks can capture the complexity of genetic
variation without the biases introduced by prior assumptions. This
approach enhances the capacity to infer discrete and continuous genetic
processes, providing a more accurate and comprehensive understanding of
the factors shaping genetic diversity. As the field of evolutionary
genetics continues to evolve, leveraging advanced techniques like
convolutional neural networks will be crucial for uncovering the
intricate details of genetic variation and evolutionary processes.

\section{Conclusion}

Integrating advanced statistical frameworks; supervised machine
learning, CNNs, and simulation-based inference, into genetic analyses
enhances our ability to quantify the drivers of genetic diversity. These
approaches enable robust, scalable, and interpretable inference across
complex landscapes and evolutionary histories. By combining traditional
population genetics theory with modern statistical learning methods,
researchers can obtain actionable insights for conservation,
biodiversity management, and understanding evolutionary processes.
